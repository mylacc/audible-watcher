Looks up each book's title and author on Google Books and Open Library. Untick to keep those lookups off; nothing else is sent to them.

Email notifications

After each check (daily, or when you click Check Audible now), Audible Watcher sends one digest email listing any new books from your watched authors and series. No new books, no email. It sends through your mail relay: an SMTP server on your own network, such as a Postfix container on your NAS, that passes the mail on to your real mail provider.

How to point it at your mail relay
  1. Mail relay: the relay's address as this container sees it. For a relay on the same NAS, use the NAS's LAN IP address, not localhost (inside a container that's the container itself), with the port the relay is published on.
  2. Security: None (plain SMTP on your own network), or STARTTLS if your relay offers it.
  3. From address: a sender your relay accepts. Relays often allow only your own domain.
  4. Send notifications to: where you want the emails.
  5. Tick “Send me an email…”, click Save settings, then click Send test email.

The relay also has to accept mail from this container's network. If it only allows certain networks, add the Docker networks to that list.

Only if your relay needs a login

Relays on your own network usually accept mail without one. Leave both blank unless yours asks for a login.

Email not working? Troubleshooting
“Could not connect” / connection refused
Check the relay address and port. From a container, use the NAS's LAN IP and the port the relay is published on, not localhost.
“Name not found”
The relay's host name has a typo, or this container can't look it up. Use its IP address instead.
“Relay access denied”, or the From or To address refused
The relay doesn't accept this sender or this container's network. Use a From address on a domain the relay allows, and allow the Docker networks in the relay's settings.
“Wrong version number”, SSL errors, or the test hangs
The security setting doesn't match the relay. A plain relay on your own network needs None.
Test says it was sent, but nothing arrives
The relay took it. Check the relay's log (for a container, docker logs on it) to see whether it was passed on or refused, and look in Spam/Junk.
